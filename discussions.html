Community Discussions

Not a comment section. You vote on your neighbors' statements, and the results show where this town actually agrees — including on things nobody expected.

Public comment rewards whoever shows up loudest and stays latest. A Livable Telluride Discussion works the other way around: everyone reads the same short statements, votes agree / disagree / pass on each one, and can add a statement of their own. Software called Pol.is then groups people by how they voted — and, more usefully, surfaces the statements that win across every group. It takes about three minutes and there is nothing to argue with.

How it works

Four steps, about three minutes.
1

Read a statement

One short sentence at a time, written by a neighbor. No threads, no replies, nobody to argue with in the margins.

2

Vote on it

Agree, disagree, or pass. Pass is a real answer — it means unsure or not my lane, and it counts.

AgreeDisagreePass
3

Add your own

Say the thing you think is missing, in one sentence. Everyone after you votes on it, so a good point spreads on its merits, not on volume.

4

See the map

As votes come in, the conversation sorts participants into opinion groups and flags the statements that hold up across all of them.

Reading the results

What the report is actually telling you.

Opinion groups

Once enough people vote, participants get sorted into two to five groups by voting pattern — not by neighborhood, party, or how long they've lived here. The groups are drawn from the data, so they usually cut across the lines everyone assumes are the real ones.

Each group gets a plain-language summary: what its members agreed on that others didn't, and where they broke from the pack.

The part that matters

  • Consensus statements — the ones that won a majority in every group. This is the short list worth bringing to a council meeting.
  • Divisive statements — the real fault lines, separated from the noise. Useful for knowing what still needs to be worked out.
  • Group-defining statements — what makes each cluster distinct.

A statement that only 40% agree with isn't a loss. Knowing it's 40% — and which 40% — is the point.

Ground rules

Same as everywhere else on this site.

Write statements, not comments

  • One idea per statement. Two ideas force people to pass.
  • Keep it short enough to vote on without re-reading.
  • Say it as a position — “Short-term rentals should be capped by neighborhood” — not as a question.
  • Don't restate someone else's statement; vote on theirs instead.

Moderation

Submitted statements are reviewed before they circulate. We remove personal attacks, statements about named private individuals, and anything off-topic — the same standard as our community guidelines. We do not remove statements for being unpopular or for disagreeing with us; that's the entire point of the exercise.

Embed contract

Polis ships one snippet per conversation:

<div class='polis' data-conversation_id='2acdmfvwwz'></div>
<script async src='https://pol.is/embed.js'></script>

embed.js scans for div.polis[data-conversation_id], replaces each with an iframe, and postMessages the content height back for auto-resize. To host more than one conversation on a site they point you at pol.is/integrate — but in practice one div per conversation on one page works, so a future list of open discussions can just stack them.
