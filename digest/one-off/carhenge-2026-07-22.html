Speak Up by Noon Wednesday: the Carhenge Subdivision and Backman Village P&Z Meeting

By Morgan Smith · July 22, 2026

This Thursday, July 23, the Town of Telluride’s Planning and Zoning Commission takes up an application that deserves more attention than it’s received. It’s called the Carhenge Preliminary Large-Scale Subdivision, and it involves Lots 34 and 34B in Backman Village. Let’s walk through what’s on the table, why it matters, and how you can weigh in before the vote.

Lots 34 and 34B, Backman Village — the parcels the Town proposes to combine.

Here’s the short version: the Town wants to remove the line between two lots and combine them into a single new parcel that will include the southern portion of the current parking area.

Staff is describing this as a tidy piece of housekeeping — no new development will be approved, just bringing the property into closer conformity with its accommodation zoning. But when you pull the thread, this is bigger than a boundary tweak. It runs straight into the recorded covenants that have governed Backman Village for years, and it raises real questions about process, authority, and fairness.

Why a “simple” lot line matters

Backman Village isn’t a blank slate. It comes with recorded covenants — legal promises attached to the land itself — that set out how these lots can be used and how much can be built. Back in 2008, a legal memo (the Geiger memorandum) concluded that those covenants require the consent of all the Backman Village owners for amendment to the subdivision plan: these covenants run with the land, they benefit the lot owners, and changing them likely requires the consent of Backman Village landowners — not just the Town and the original developer.

That’s the crux. You can redraw a boundary on a plat, but redrawing a line doesn’t automatically rewrite the covenants, the density rules, or the use restrictions baked into those lots. If combining the parcels quietly changes what’s allowed in Backman Village without the consent the covenants require, then this isn’t housekeeping — it’s a change to people’s vested rights done by replat alone. We do not know the Town’s position regarding the legal effect of this lot line vacation.

The questions the Commission needs to answer first

Before anyone votes to approve, condition, continue, or deny this, the record should clearly show that these questions have been asked and answered — in writing, on the record:

  • Consent. Do the Backman Village covenants even allow this consolidation without owner consent? The Chair 7 Community Coalition raised exactly this point in their own July 21 letter.
  • Authority. Will this subdivision action be used to alter recorded, lot-specific covenants and density allocations at all? Redrawing boundaries and rewriting rights are two different things. Will the Town take the position that this subdivision supersedes all prior legal requirements? Who knows.
  • Who’s advising whom. This is a quasi-judicial hearing, which means the Commission is supposed to act like an impartial judge based on the record. So it’s fair to ask whether the Town attorney is advising the P&Z on the law while also advocating for the Town’s own application. That dual role, even if well-intentioned, creates at least the appearance of a conflict — and appearances matter when public trust is on the line.
  • What changed since 2008. The current position seems to point in a different direction than that earlier Geiger memo. If the Town is departing from its own prior legal analysis, the members of the community deserve a straight answer: what changed in the facts, what changed in the law, is the old interpretation being abandoned, and is there a fresh, independent legal opinion behind the new one?

The right course of action

Before final action, the Planning and Zoning Commission should continue the matter until an independent legal review is complete that advises the Commission on whether:

  1. this vacation of a lot line and creation of a new subdivision is, or is not, consistent with the existing Backman Village subdivision covenants;
  2. a written legal analysis of the covenant and plat issues should be put into the record;
  3. or the application should be denied without prejudice (meaning it can be applied for again) if the legal footing for the lot line vacation simply isn’t there yet.

The bottom line is simple. Based on the issues before it, the P&Z Commission shouldn’t approve a lot line removal and a new subdivision inside Backman Village until the legal effect of these covenants and the question of owner consent have all been addressed openly and on the record.

The hearing is Thursday, July 23, 2026, at 5:30 p.m., before the Planning and Zoning Commission in Rebekah Hall, 113 W. Columbia Avenue. It’s a hybrid meeting, so you can also attend remotely via the link on the Town’s agenda.

  • Show up. Whether you support this project, oppose any development at this location, or are somewhere in between, your public presence sends a message that a written comment can’t.
  • Submit a comment. Written comments become part of the public record. Even a few sentences to the P&Z about whether you believe independent legal analysis should occur — send it to the Town Clerk and ask that it be entered into the record for the July 23 Carhenge hearing. Send it as soon as you can so the commissioners have time to read it before Thursday evening.
  • Spread the word. Forward this to a neighbor who cares about how this region grows and who gets a say in it.
