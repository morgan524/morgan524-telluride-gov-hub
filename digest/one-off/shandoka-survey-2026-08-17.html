Last Call on the Shandoka Survey

By Morgan Smith · August 17, 2026

This email is a quick update on our online survey, which has received a great response. Thank you all so far.

In light of the HARC meeting on Wednesday, we plan to submit the response by noon on Tuesday, leaving only a small window for anyone to take the survey and share their opinions. We are primarily interested in people who live in the area, but it is open to anyone, since projects of this size can affect or benefit everyone in the region. You can add your answers and thoughts to our survey.

Closes at noon, Tuesday, August 18.

This project appears to be on a rocket ship headed for approval, and HARC has the final word before the Town Council decides whether to green-light it. Yet again, the HARC package does not include any construction cost estimates in the 420-page document, so your guess is as good as mine on how it all pencils out. HARC’s review does not assess whether this project is a good idea or financially viable, but simply whether it fits within the community design and scale.

One issue squarely before the reviewing bodies is whether to grant the PUD dimensional variation Building 1 requires. The main garage and residential building comes in at 210,312 SF Total against a 30,000 SF-per-structure limit — essentially seven times the stated maximum building size for this location.

Floor area is only one of three. Staff also find Building 1 exceeds the 40-foot mid-height limit at 46’-7” (49’-11” to the highest ridge), and that Buildings 1 and 2 cover 39.13% of the site against a 30% limit. On each one, the staff conclusion is the same: “Building 2 complies. Building 1 requires a PUD dimensional variation.”

The changes and plans are moving so fast; we apologize that the image in the original survey showed a prior iteration of the proposal. We wanted to update everyone with more current images of the proposal that will be before HARC. We’ve updated that image on the survey page.

Here is a top view of the proposal, with housing surrounding the parking structure, about 818 parking spaces in the main parking structure of Building 1, and another 68 parking spaces below Buildings 2, 3, and 4.

But to reduce overall height, the plans now call for about two and a half levels of below-grade parking, roughly 30 feet down, which will not be cheap to build.

“The parking facility has been reconfigured to provide approximately 2.5 levels of below-grade parking. This approach relocates an upper-level ramp and creates usable green space on the uppermost level of the deck.”
— Applicant response to HARC, August 19, 2026 packet

The plans still call for about 56 residential units, but the current design creates a space between the parking structure and “Building 2,” which is closest to the river in the drawing below.

That is a summary of the current project, love it or hate it. Our survey includes a space for anyone to write whatever they like in support of or opposition to this proposal.

Now is the time to express an opinion, no later than Tuesday at noon, as there is a high likelihood HARC will approve the development, leaving the final decision to the Town Council. As with prior projects, the funding will likely be structured so that, while the Town is on the hook for the debt, it does not trigger a vote of the electorate under TABOR. In short, your window to make comments is quickly closing.
