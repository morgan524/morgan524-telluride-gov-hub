Livable Telluride · Weekly Update
The Week Ahead Outlook
November 16 - November 22, 2026
📅 The Week Ahead Outlook

Monday, the valley's public meeting schedule opens with three late-afternoon gatherings before moving into the evening. The SMART Gondola Advisory Committee meets at 3:00 PM, followed at 3:30 PM by the Telluride Board of Education Work Session, and the San Miguel County Open Space Commission convenes at 4:00 PM. That evening at 6:00 PM, the Ouray City Council takes up the full 2027 budget cycle — four linked resolutions covering budget adoption, fund appropriations, the mill levy, and the 2027 fee schedule. Also Monday, with no posted time, the Norwood Planning and Zoning Commission works on two foundational documents: a Land Use Code update and a Master Plan update.

Tuesday is the week's busiest day. The Ouray County Board of County Commissioners opens at 9:00 AM with two notable resolutions: one repealing the county's existing austerity measures and another adopting a formal AI policy and governance standards, one of the first such moves in the region. At 10:00 AM, the Telluride Town Council votes on the 2027 budget and the property tax levy that funds it; woven into that same session is second reading of an ordinance suspending the Open Space Fund's 5% share of Real Estate Transfer Tax revenue for the 2027 budget year ��� a recurring tension between fiscal pressure and land-conservation commitments — along with second reading of amendments to the Telluride Energy Mitigation Program and an update to its energy offset fee calculator. Also at 10:05 AM, the Telluride (Master Lease Lottery) Telluride Housing Authority conducts lottery drawings for two Black Bear Road properties: the Telluride Boarding House and the Virginia Placer IIA Apartments. Tuesday evening brings the Telluride Board of Education Monthly Meeting at 5:15 PM and the Fire District Board of Directors Meeting at 5:30 PM. The Ophir General Assembly also meets Tuesday, with no posted time.

Wednesday morning, the Ouray County Board of County Commissioners Work Session opens at 9:00 AM with an EMS director quarterly update and a discussion of an intergovernmental agreement between the county and Ridgway Fire. The San Miguel County Board of County Commissioners Meeting follows at 9:30 AM with no agenda detail posted beyond the location. At noon, the Telluride Parks & Recreation Commission takes up a proposed update to the Town Park Pavilion Banner Policy that would add a category for Telluride high school senior winter-sports athletes. Wednesday evening, Telluride HARC convenes at 5:30 PM for a heavy agenda: preliminary large-scale reviews for the Town-owned Shandoka Lot redevelopment at 860 Black Bear Rd — Buildings 1 & 2 and Buildings 3 & 4, with Design Workshop as the design firm — along with preliminary large-scale reviews for two other Town-owned sites, 113 W Columbia (demolition of the existing principal structure and large-scale new construction at Rebekah Hall) and 335 W Colorado (demolition of a historically rated primary structure, large-scale new construction, and relocation of two historic sheds). The Rico Board of Trustees holds its regular meeting Wednesday at 7:00 PM.

Thursday, the Medical Center Regular Board Meeting runs from 8:30 AM to 11:30 AM, and the TRAA Board of Commissioners Meeting follows at noon. The Mountain Village Town Council convenes at 3:00 PM for a full session: a public hearing on the Mountain Village Metro District's proposed 2027 budget, first readings of the 2027 Town budget ordinance and 2026 property tax levy ordinance, and adoption of 2027 fee schedules. Two work sessions carry particular weight — one on the draft sewer authority agreement between Mountain Village and Telluride for a proposed regional wastewater treatment plant, and one on the Rural Homes Ilium affordable housing feasibility study — before the meeting closes with an executive session on the ongoing Sunset Concert litigation. Also Thursday at 4:00 PM, the Telluride Resident Advisory Committee meets. At 5:30 PM, the Telluride Planning & Zoning Commission hears a presentation on wildfire modeling technologies from Firebird Chief Scientist Jason Sibold, continues a work session on potential Land Use Code amendments to landscaping and tree-removal rules, and elects a new chair and vice chair.

A heavy agenda for November 18. THU, NOV 19 · 5:30 PMTown of Telluride
Town of Telluride Planning & Zoning Commission
A light agenda for this one. The 222 West San Juan Avenue subdivision work session — which had been continued from October — has since been withdrawn by the applicant, so it won't be heard. The commission will get a presentation on wildfire modeling technologies from Firebird Chief Scientist Jason Sibold, and continue a work session on potential Land Use Code amendments to landscaping requirements (LUC 3-502) and tree maintenance and removal rules (LUC 3-505). The board will also elect a new chair and vice chair, hear about the December 3 Comprehensive Plan community engagement session, and review the draft 2027 meeting calendar.
ℹ Why This Matters
A Comprehensive Plan revision sets the rules governing all future land use and zoning — changes that can reshape development patterns for decades. This is one of the most consequential items any planning body considers. Public input at this stage carries the most weight; once formal review begins, options narrow quickly.
