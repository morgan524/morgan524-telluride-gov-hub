Livable Telluride · Weekly Update
The Week Ahead Outlook
November 16 - November 22, 2026
📅 The Week Ahead Outlook

Monday, the valley's public calendar opens with several afternoon sessions. The SMART Gondola Advisory Committee meets at 3:00 PM, followed at 3:30 PM by the Telluride Board of Education Work Session, and then the San Miguel County Open Space Commission Meeting 11/23 at 4:00 PM. The Ouray City Council Regular Meeting follows at 6:00 PM; no agenda has been posted yet. The Norwood Planning and Zoning Commission Meeting is also Monday, with no posted time or agenda.

Tuesday is the busiest day of the week. The Ouray County Board of County Commissioners opens at 9:00 AM with no posted agenda. At 10:00 AM, the Telluride Town Council takes up its most consequential session in a while: a vote on the 2027 budget and the property tax levy that funds it, second reading of an ordinance that would suspend the Open Space Fund's 5% share of Real Estate Transfer Tax revenue for the year — a real tension between fiscal pressure and long-standing land conservation commitments — and second reading of amendments to the Telluride Energy Mitigation Program alongside an update to the energy offset fee calculator, with two executive sessions to close. Just five minutes later at 10:05 AM, the Telluride (Master Lease Lottery) Telluride Housing Authority holds lottery drawings for two Black Bear Road rental properties. The Ouray County Board of County Commissioners Work Session follows at 1:30 PM. The Telluride Board of Education Monthly Meeting meets at 5:15 PM, and the Fire District Board of Directors Meeting at 5:30 PM. The Ophir General Assembly Meeting is also Tuesday, with no posted time or agenda.

Wednesday brings the Ouray County Board of County Commissioners Work Session at 9:00 AM and the San Miguel County Board of County Commissioners Meeting at 9:30 AM — the agenda lists only the location, with no item detail posted. The Telluride Parks & Recreation Commission meets at noon. At 5:30 PM, the Telluride HARC Meeting carries a substantial agenda: the long-delayed preliminary large-scale reviews for the Shandoka Lot redevelopment at 860 Black Bear Rd are finally on the docket, with the Town of Telluride as applicant on all four buildings; also up are preliminary reviews for two other town-owned sites — demolition and new construction at 113 W Columbia (Rebekah Hall) and demolition of a historic-rated structure plus large-scale new construction at 335 W Colorado. The Rico Board of Trustees Regular Meeting closes out Wednesday at 7:00 PM.

Thursday, the TRAA Board of Commissioners Meeting meets at noon. The Mountain Village Town Council Meeting follows at 3:00 PM, and the Telluride Resident Advisory Committee at 4:00 PM. At 5:30 PM, the Telluride Planning & Zoning Commission has a lighter agenda but a few items worth watching: a presentation on wildfire modeling technologies from Firebird Chief Scientist Jason Sibold, a continued work session on potential Land Use Code amendments to landscaping and tree maintenance rules, election of a new chair and vice chair, and a preview of the December 3 Comprehensive Plan community engagement session.

WED, NOV 18 · 5:30 PMTown of Telluride
Town of Telluride HARC Meeting
A heavy agenda for November 18. The two items that have been waiting since August — the Shandoka Lot redevelopment preliminary large-scale reviews for Buildings 1 & 2 and Buildings 3 & 4 at 860 Black Bear Rd — are finally being heard, with the Town of Telluride as applicant and Design Workshop doing the design work. Both involve new construction outside the historic district exceeding 5,000 sq ft in an Accommodations 2 zone.
ℹ Why This Matters
A proposed 900-space parking garage at Shandoka would be one of the largest structures in the region. Combined with Chair 7 and the gondola redesign, these projects represent a massive escalation in commercial infrastructure. THU, NOV 19 · 5:30 PMTown of Telluride
Town of Telluride Planning & Zoning Commission
A light agenda for this one. The 222 West San Juan Avenue subdivision work session — which had been continued from October — has since been withdrawn by the applicant, so it won't be heard. The commission will get a presentation on wildfire modeling technologies from Firebird Chief Scientist Jason Sibold, and continue a work session on potential Land Use Code amendments to landscaping requirements (LUC 3-502) and tree maintenance and removal rules (LUC 3-505). The board will also elect a new chair and vice chair, hear about the December 3 Comprehensive Plan community engagement session, and review the draft 2027 meeting calendar.
ℹ Why This Matters
A Comprehensive Plan revision sets the rules governing all future land use and zoning — changes that can reshape development patterns for decades. This is one of the most consequential items any planning body considers. Public input at this stage carries the most weight; once formal review begins, options narrow quickly.
