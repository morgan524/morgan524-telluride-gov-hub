# The Hotel Externality Model — public cost per approved visitor bed (2026-08-01)

Quantifies what a lodging/commercial approval in one jurisdiction costs the
public region-wide: unmet worker-housing need, public capital per unit and
per hotel key, and the draw on shared wastewater and road capacity. Worked
example: Four Seasons Telluride + Six Senses 109R (both Mountain Village;
neither Telluride nor the County had approval authority). Interactive
version: `assets/Housing Study/project-impact-calculator.html`.

Grading follows the baseline convention (A/B/C/X, [E] = extrapolation with
stated method). Every factor below is a RANGE; conclusions are presented as
ranges and do not depend on point choices.

## The five-link chain

1. **Jobs.** Luxury-tier hotels staff at 1.5–2.5 employees per key [E,
   industry convention; see report endnote 66]. Commercial space at the
   Telluride LUC rate, 2.5 jobs/1,000 sq ft [A, adopted code rate].
   - Four Seasons: ~43 hotel keys + 26 serviced private residences
     (five-star service program; no on-site EH identified) [B, developer
     materials].
   - Six Senses: ~81 lodge units (50 hot-bed + 31 lodge) + 20 condos +
     ~26,000 sq ft commercial; 20 on-site EH units (18 dorms 306–408 sq ft
     + 2 apartments) [A, MV DRB packet 12/2022].
   - Combined: **200–400 new service jobs** [E].
2. **Jobs → workers.** Resort workers hold ~1.2 jobs each [E, standard
   mountain-resort HNA convention; cross-check against SMC 2025 HNA
   methodology when quoting].
3. **Workers → households.** 1.4–1.8 workers per household [E, same
   convention family].
4. **Less on-site housing.** Six Senses dorms+apts ≈ 25–40 workers; Four
   Seasons 0.
5. **Gap × real public delivery cost** (from evidence/subsidy-by-ami.md;
   service wages ⇒ 60–100% AMI tier):
   - Sunnyside-class delivery ($516k TDC/unit, free public land): public
     gap **$145k–$352k/unit**
   - VooDoo-class delivery ($1.26M TDC/unit): **$678k–$1.09M/unit**

## Worked result (Four Seasons + Six Senses)

| Step | Low | High | Central |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jobs | 200 | 400 | ~300 |
| Workers (÷1.2) | 167 | 333 | ~250 |
| Less on-site | −40 | −25 | −30 |
| Households (÷1.8 / ÷1.4) | ~70 | ~220 | **~140** |
| Public capital | $10M | $240M | **$55–100M** |

**Per-bed metric (the communicating number):** central bill ÷ ~124 keys ≈
**$450k–$800k of unfunded public housing obligation per luxury hotel key**;
at ~2.2 pillows/key, **$200k–$360k per guest pillow**. Approved by one
jurisdiction; the liability lands on all.

## Shared-system draw

- **Wastewater:** ~275 guest pillows ≈ 275 person-equivalents at peak;
  F&B/spa/laundry high-strength load pushes to ~400–600 PE; plus ~250
  workers wherever they live in the service area. At 70–100 gal/person/day
  ⇒ **~0.05–0.08 MGD ≈ 5–8% of the new Ilium plant's entire ~1.0–1.1 MGD
  generational headroom** (report endnote 62 arithmetic).
- **Traffic:** ~200+ commuting workers (Society Turn/Last Dollar pair or
  gondola) + resort generation ~5 trips/key/day ≈ **~600 daily trips**.
  Yardstick from the Diamond Ridge study [A]: one 240-unit project advances
  Society Turn roundabout failure to ~2027 — this is a same-order increment,
  and each hotel's traffic study was run in isolation.

## The instrument to institutionalize

A standing one-page **"Project Housing & Infrastructure Bill"** published
for every approval region-wide (extends report Recommendation R2): jobs by
wage tier → net unhoused households by AMI → public capital at demonstrated
delivery costs → MGD/BOD draw → peak trips → gondola/evacuation load.
Precedents: Aspen/Pitkin adopted employee-generation rates; Whistler
bed-unit ledger. Report Section 7.2 carries the narrative version; the
calculator makes it reproducible for any future project.
