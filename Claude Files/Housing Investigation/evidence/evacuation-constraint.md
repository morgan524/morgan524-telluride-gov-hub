# The evacuation constraint (2026-07-31)

**Source:** San Miguel County All-Hazard Mitigation Plan 2023 [official;
local: `assets/Housing Study/Emergency/2023 AHMP (PDF) (A)_...pdf`]. Peer
frame: Sanibel FL grounded its ~9,000-unit build-out cap partly in hurricane
evacuation clearance time (see peer-carrying-capacity-frameworks.md).

## What the County's own plan says

- **Telluride:** "located within a box canyon and is accessed by one road."
  Rockfall or in-town flooding "would very quickly snarl traffic and cut off
  access." Hwy 145 — the only highway linking the eastern county — is
  "susceptible to mud/debris flows and rockfall and is often closed for
  short periods" in monsoon season; extended closure isolates Telluride,
  Mountain Village, and Ophir except via Dolores County.
- **Wildfire evacuation is "a huge concern":** three highways total in the
  county; "many communities and neighborhoods only have single points of
  ingress/egress"; narrow access roads mean firefighting apparatus and
  evacuating traffic compete head-on. The plan rates the county **"highly
  vulnerable"** to a significant wildfire.
- **Festival exposure:** life safety flagged specifically because of
  "limited evacuation routes and high influx of visitors to the eastern
  County during summer festivals" — peak population, not resident
  population, is the evacuation load.
- **Mountain Village's own mitigation action #17** is a *secondary
  ingress/egress road* (wildfire) — the jurisdictions already concede the
  egress gap in writing.
- **Ophir:** in winter, highways are the only evacuation routes and one
  avalanche closes both directions.

## The gap that matters for carrying capacity

The AHMP is entirely **qualitative** on evacuation: it contains **no
clearance-time model** — no estimate of how many hours it takes to empty the
box canyon at today's population, let alone at build-out or festival peak.
Sanibel's legally durable cap rested precisely on such a number.

**Implication (for the white paper):** every additional East End unit and
every additional festival bed adds evacuation load to a two-node road system
(Society Turn RAB + Last Dollar/145 — the same intersections the traffic
studies show failing on background growth). A defensible carrying-capacity
framework should make an **evacuation clearance-time study** the first
gating analysis: it converts "how much housing is safe?" from an opinion
into an engineering number, on a public-safety foundation that HB23-1255
does not preempt.
