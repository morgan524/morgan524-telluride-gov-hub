# Demand vs. Vacancy Evidence — Subsidized Rental Housing, Telluride / Mountain Village

*Compiled 2026-07-31. Regime marker: **[PRE]** = before income-based rents took effect Jan 1, 2025; **[POST]** = after. (The 25%/27% multiplier revision effective 2026-01-01 and the rent formula itself are documented elsewhere — this file is demand/vacancy evidence only.)*

**Headline numbers:** Voodoo rental lottery Aug 2024: 33 households for 27 units (1.2:1). Town rentals March 2026: 49 of 201 vacant (~24%). SMRHA ownership lottery Apr 2026: 18 households for 3 units (6:1). VCA (below-market rents): waitlist capped since 2020 at ~1,000.

## 2. Chronological evidence log

**Fall 2023 [PRE]** — Town compliance survey: "more than 57% of tenants were not meeting existing qualifications" (Telluride Inside, Nov 2024, on the 244 town-constructed units); Colorado Sun puts it at ~50% in three of five complexes, "mostly because they were earning too much to qualify." Trigger for the policy overhaul.

**Nov 2, 2023 [PRE]** — Daily Planet, "Public asked to try again for apartment waitlist": VCA waitlist held ~1,000 applicants when it closed in 2020; MVHA reopened a studio-only list Nov 8, 2023, limited to the first 50 applications in a two-hour window. Demand rationing, not absence of demand.

**Jul 24 – Aug 29, 2024 [PRE]** — Voodoo initial lottery (Town CivicAlert AID=282): applications noon Jul 24 to noon Aug 16; drawing Aug 29 at Rebekah Hall; required 3+ years employment in Telluride R-1 School District. The certified "Voodoo Lottery Drawing Placement — August 29, 2024" table (DocumentCenter 14685) contains **70 drawing entries across applicant numbers 1–33 — i.e., 33 applicant households for 27 brand-new units (~1.2:1)**. Compare SMRHA's ownership lottery 20 months later at 6:1. Even before any income-based rent existed, rental demand at Voodoo's 110–170% AMI price point was nearly saturated by supply.

**Nov 2024 [PRE]** — Council + Telluride Housing Authority adopt new Employee Rental Housing Policies (Telluride Inside): income-based tiered rents, eligibility to 240% AMI, 1,400 hr/yr work requirement, implementation with lease renewals beginning **January 2025**.

**Dec 18, 2024 [boundary]** — Town CivicAlert AID=312 (now 404; Wayback capture 2025-04-03): "The Town of Telluride has opened applications for a limited number of remaining units in the Voodoo Employee Rental Housing Project… first-come, first-served basis through 5 pm on Tuesday, December 31." Units: one studio at $2,301; three 3BRs (one $2,961, two $3,455); one 4BR at $3,562. "Eligibility for new applications will require only one year of employment history… expanding eligibility to a broader audience." DeLanie Tapson: "we anticipate strong interest in this limited opportunity." **Read: 5 of 27 brand-new units unclaimed ~4 months after the lottery, at rents targeting 110–170% AMI.**

**Jan 2025 [POST]** — Income-based rents begin at renewals. Effects tallied later by the Colorado Sun: 82 households saw increases (35 of them >49%), 35 decreases, 57 unchanged. Cases: $1,800→$2,400→$3,600 (Sanchez); $2,450→$2,850→$4,400 (Rineer); $2,200→$2,575→$4,000 (nonprofit employee).

**Dec 12, 2024 / 2025 rates [POST]** — MV Town Council approved VCA 2025 rates: +5% over 2024, new tier for Phases I–III tenants at 100%+ AMI (Daily Planet). VCA waitlist remains capped.

**Feb 2026 [POST]** — Telluride council adjustments: teenage income removed from calculations, work-hours requirement reduced; Housing Director DeLanie Tapson tells council the vacancy rate "is not because of a lack of demand." Councilman Dan Enright: "Nearly 50 vacancies for at least six months now… two facts seem irreconcilable." (Colorado Sun. Note Enright's "six months" places the ~50-unit vacancy back to roughly Sept–Oct 2025.)

**Mar 17 / Mar 30, 2026 [POST]** — Telluride Housing Authority adopts temporary changes (telluride.gov NewsFlash 386): work-hours requirement cut 1,400→1,200 for 2026 leases (resort closure + low-snow season); under-18 income permanently excluded. Board Chair Elena Levin: adjustments "recognize the real impacts that recent events have had on our community."

**Mar→Apr 2026 [POST]** — Town reports **49 empty units of 201** (~24% vacancy; normal mountain-town range 2–5%). ~15 of 49 under renovation. Mayor Teddy Errico: vacancy rate is "unacceptable." Apr 8: town emails a "very stale waitlist" offering units first-come-first-served regardless of list position. Tri-County Health Network (Mar 30): "consistent and concerning feedback" from residents. 2025 revenue for context: Shandoka (123 u) $2.6M; Voodoo (27 u) $872K; Sunnyside (30 u) $675K. (Colorado Sun, Apr 19, 2026.)

**Apr 24, 2026 [POST]** — SMRHA purchase lottery (certified results + qualified list, smrha.org): **18 qualified households, 26 drawing entries, for 3 deed-restricted ownership units** priced $352K–$405K — Silver Jack 202 (6 entrants), Silver Jack 205 (16), Element 52 SW-102 (7). Two units closed to new owners; SJ 202 pending as of the site update. **Ownership demand is ~6 households per unit at the same moment ~24% of town rentals sit empty.**

**Oct 14 + Oct 28, 2025 → Jan 1, 2026 [POST]** — CivicAlert AID=355 (posted Oct 14, 2025): council directs staff to cut the rent-to-income multiplier 30%→27% (Virginia Placer, Sunnyside, Voodoo, Shandoka F) and 27%→25% (Shandoka A–E, G–H), effective Jan 1, 2026 — expected savings **$100–$400/month per household, up to $900 for some tenants**. Councilmember Elena Levin: "we have reserves, and we subsidize housing to make it affordable. This is the right time to use those reserves to support residents." Formal adoption reviewed Oct 28, 2025 (AID=359), with Tier D (100% AMI) floor at Voodoo and an amenities multiplier. (Formula detail documented elsewhere; included here as the town's revealed-preference admission that POST rents overshot demand — and note the size of the retreat, up to $900/month, as a proxy for how far.)

## 3. What this reveals about the demand curve

1. **Demand is price-elastic and the town found the wrong side of the curve.** At below-market legacy rents [PRE], every proxy shows queueing: ~1,000 households on VCA's waitlist, waitlist caps and 50-application windows, >50% of town tenants clinging to units they no longer qualified for. At income-based rents reaching $3,600–$4,400 [POST], the same stock runs ~24% vacant and the waitlist went "stale."

2. **Voodoo bracketed the price point early.** Even PRE-regime, only 33 households entered the lottery for 27 units (~1.2:1), and the 110–170% AMI initial rents still left 5 of 27 new units unleased into late December 2024, forcing an eligibility cut from 3 years to 1 year of local employment. The thin end of demand sits well below $3,455 for a 3BR — a signal available to the town a full year before the Oct 2025 multiplier retreat. Contrast the lottery ratios: rental at near-market rents 1.2:1 (Aug 2024) vs. deed-restricted ownership 6:1 (Apr 2026).

3. **The vacancy is a price/rules phenomenon, not a demand shortage.** The town's own housing director said so on the record ("not because of a lack of demand"), the 2025 needs assessment says 1,114 units are needed by 2034, and 18 qualified households chased 3 ownership units in April 2026. Households will queue for years for a capped asset (deed-restricted purchase, VCA legacy rents) while declining income-contingent rents that approach or exceed free-market equivalents once annual income re-verification risk is priced in.

4. **The policy retreat traces the demand curve in real time:** 30% multiplier launches (Jan 2025) → council directs 27%/25% multipliers, Tier-D floor at Voodoo, amenities multiplier (Oct 14/28, 2025; effective Jan 1, 2026; up to $900/month relief) → teenage-income exclusion + work-hours cut 1,400→1,200 (Feb–Mar 2026, post resort closure) → first-come-first-served unit offers ignoring the waitlist (Apr 2026). Each step is the town bidding rent expectations down toward where demand actually clears — and vacancies were still ~49 in March 2026, two-plus months after the multiplier cut took effect.

5. **Comparators show the outlier status:** Summit County vacancy <7%, Eagle ~2% with 1–3 year workforce waitlists, against Telluride's ~24% — same macro environment, different pricing rule.
