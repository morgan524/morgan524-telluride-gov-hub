## Section 1 — Telluride Regional Wastewater Treatment Plant (TRWWTP): current status

### 1(a) Discharge permit and rated capacities

- **Permit:** CDPS/NPDES permit **CO0041840**, "Telluride Regional WWTF," San Miguel County, CO. Status in EPA ECHO (retrieved 2026-07-31): **Admin Continued** — the permit **expired 11/30/2025** and is administratively continued while CDPHE processes renewal. [official — EPA ECHO detailed facility report, CO0041840, https://echo.epa.gov/detailed-facility-report?fid=CO0041840; data via echodata.epa.gov CWA REST services, retrieved 2026-07-31]
- **Compliance (retrieved 2026-07-31):** ECHO shows current status **"Violation Identified"**, with **12 of the last 13 quarters in noncompliance**, including **7 quarters in Significant/Category I Noncompliance (SNC)**. Parameters flagged in the 3-year record include **zinc (potentially dissolved)** and **total residual chlorine**. Last CWA inspection **07/17/2024** (2 in the 5-yr window). [official — EPA ECHO, CO0041840, retrieved 2026-07-31]
- **Rated hydraulic capacity:** the June 2025 three-party MOU states the 2017 Stantec Regional Wastewater Master Plan called for expanding the Existing Plant "from **2.1 MGD to 2.3 MGD**" — i.e., the plant remains rated ~**2.1 MGD**; the Town's website likewise says it processes "up to 2.1 million gallons." [official — Regional WWTP MOU recitals, MV council packet 2025-06-18, https://townofmountainvillage.com/site/assets/files/46518/june_18-_2025_consideration_of_a_memorandum_of_understanding_between_san_miguel_county-_the_town_of_mountain_village_and_the.pdf; Town of Telluride, https://www.telluride-co.gov/265/Water-Wastewater-Treatment-Plant]
- **Capacity trigger status:** Engage Telluride (Town project page, retrieved 2026-07-31) states the plant **has reached the 80% capacity threshold** that requires expansion planning under its CDPS permit, and that staff are "measuring and reporting BOD concentrations in the inflow to the plant that triggers both engineering and construction requirements" (construction required at 95%). The binding constraint is organic (BOD) loading, as the 2017 Master Plan projected. [official — https://engagetelluride.org/telluride-regional-wastewater-treatment-plant]

### 1(b) Upgrades completed/budgeted since 2017 (dollar amounts)

Timeline from Engage Telluride "Key Dates" [official — https://engagetelluride.org/telluride-regional-wastewater-treatment-plant/key_dates, retrieved 2026-07-31]:

- **Nov 2022:** Design Camp + geotechnical field investigation for on-site expansion (design-build).
- **Apr–May 2023:** Basis of Design Report prepared for CDPHE; permitting begun with CDPHE and San Miguel County.
- **Jun 2023:** 30% engineered design package reviewed with Design-Build team.
- **Jan 2024:** **Design-Build contract CANCELED** — expansion costs exceeded budget and the existing site couldn't accommodate future expansion. Cost basis: upgrading the current plant estimated at **over $133 million**, adding only **0.2 MGD** and meeting regional needs only through 2047 (figures presented at the 2025-03-19 BOCC work session).
- **Apr 2024:** engineering team selected for small life-extension improvements at the existing plant.
- **May 2024:** Source Control Plan implemented (reduce unacceptable wastes entering the plant).
- **Aug 2024:** replacement equipment selected for **failing headworks** components; contract finalization planned 2025.

Town of Telluride 2025 Budget Book — Wastewater Fund capital [official — 2025 Budget Book PDF, https://telluride.gov/DocumentCenter/View/15248/2025-Town-of-Telluride-Budget-Book, adopted 2024-11-19]:

- **2025 itemized wastewater capital requests — $9.7M total:**
  - Treatment Plant Improvements **$5,500,000** (replace failing headworks equipment, redundant dewatering screw press, new influent pump station and equalization tank, lab renovation)
  - Treatment Plant Engineering **$2,000,000** ("Planning and Engineering to construct a new Telluride Regional Wastewater Treatment Plant (TRWWTP)")
  - Wastewater System Improvements **$1,800,000** (Telluride Lodge sewer main relocation, I&I repairs, manhole lining)
  - Biosolids truck/trailer **$400,000**
- **5-year (2025–2030) wastewater CIP — $139.1M total**, dominated by "Treatment Plant Improvements" at **$132.5M** plus $3.2M new-plant engineering and $3.0M system improvements. (The $132.5M line is the placeholder for the plant rebuild/replacement.)
- 2025 Wastewater Fund total expenditures budgeted at **$13.73M** (vs. $3.58M actual in FY2023). Mine Tax Fund transferred **$2.1M (2024)** to the Wastewater Fund "towards paying debt on the wastewater treatment plant improvements," with remaining balance reserved for future gondola and WWTP capital.
- 14.0 FTE budgeted in Wastewater (2024 and 2025). [official — 2025 Budget Book staffing chart]
- **Rates:** 2023 Burns & McDonnell rate study called for wastewater rate increases of **18% in 2025, 18%/yr 2026–2027, 4% in 2028** (the 18% 2025 increase was applied); because "the scope of the Wastewater Treatment plant has changed considerably since this study was completed," staff recommended only **5% for 2026** (first-reading ordinance, fall 2025). [official — Town of Telluride 2026 Water & Wastewater Rate Ordinance council memo, https://telluride-co.civicweb.net/document/426357/; 2025 Budget Book p. "Charges for Services"]
- **NOT FOUND:** the dollar amount in the Nov 2020 "Council endorses funds for treatment plant upgrades" article (https://www.telluridenews.com/news/article_24afd4f4-1a2e-11eb-812c-433f92d20677.html) — telluridenews.com returned HTTP 429 on both fetch attempts. [news, unverified]

### 1(c) Formal plans for a new regional plant (Society Turn context → Ilium site)

- **Active site is NOT Society Turn.** In **June 2024** (contract executed 2024-06-19) Mountain Village put a **5-acre parcel under contract for $1.1 million** from the Alexander Family — "along the San Miguel River west of Ilium Valley," adjacent to the San Miguel County/CDOT shops off Highway 145 — for the future regional plant. The Alexanders hold a buy-back option if construction hasn't commenced within 7 years of closing. [official — MV Town Manager memo 2025-06-18, URL above; news — Telluride Daily Planet 2024-07, https://www.telluridenews.com/news/article_43d22eda-3e43-11ef-995c-8fbf6cdb6bf8.html]
- **June 2025 — Regional Wastewater Treatment Plant MOU** among San Miguel County, Mountain Village, and Telluride (MV council considered 2025-06-18; County and Telluride June 2025 meetings). Key terms [official — MOU text in MV packet, URL above]:
  - The Towns intend to form a **Sewer Authority** to construct, own, and operate a new **RWWTP**; MOU auto-terminates if no Authority agreement by **July 1, 2028**.
  - **Target capacity ~2.67 MGD** — sized for existing buildout, committed users (e.g., Ilium Valley contracts), plus septage acceptance and future unincorporated-county connections.
  - County gets a **non-voting advisory seat**; septage generated in the county to be accepted; differential rates contemplated for **deed-restricted housing** and low/very-low/fixed-income customers.
  - Recitals confirm: existing plant built **1987**, expanded **1994 and 2002**; the Towns have been evaluating CDPHE-driven options **since 2010**.
- **Cost share:** Mountain Village states it contributes **35% of capital and operating expenses in exchange for 35% of treatment capacity** at the regional plant. [official — Town of Mountain Village project page, https://townofmountainvillage.com/government/town-projects/regional-wastewater-treatment-plant-project/, via search snippet (page returns 403 to bots), retrieved 2026-07-31]
- Process milestones: BOCC work session 2025-03-19 (site feasibility + $133M/0.2 MGD upgrade economics); BOCC work session on the authority 2025-05-14; MV pre-application meeting with the County 2024-11-19. [official — MV memo + Engage Telluride]
- 2025 Town Council goals include: formulate the regional Wastewater Authority agreement; comprehensive funding plan with Mountain Village; update the Wastewater Master Plan; and "depending on the direction of a new regional WWTP, initiate planning for new public facilities at the current WWTP site." [official — 2025 Budget Book, Goals & Objectives]

### 1(d) Recent reported flows/loads and capacity statements

- **Current demand (June 2025, "Towns' most recent studies"): average ~0.8 MGD, average peaks ~1.6 MGD** against the ~2.1 MGD hydraulic rating. [official — Regional WWTP MOU, Term 1, June 2025]
- The plant is nonetheless **at/above the 80% trigger on BOD (organic) loading**, per the Town — i.e., the plant is load-limited, not flow-limited. [official — Engage Telluride, retrieved 2026-07-31]
- ECHO's multi-year SNC record (zinc, chlorine residual) independently corroborates a plant straining against its permit. [official — EPA ECHO, CO0041840]

## Section 2 — Operating-cost benchmarks: mature resort housing programs

*(Purpose: projecting the long-run operating cost of Telluride's program — Town now holds ~251 rental units incl. VooDoo; the 2025 Budget Book, written pre-VooDoo-stabilization, describes 185 rental units + a 46-bed boardinghouse.)*

### 2(a) APCHA (Aspen-Pitkin County Housing Authority)

- **2025 budget: just under $10 million**, adding 1 FTE (Housing Policy Analyst II, +$127,390). **~$1.9M** is main-office administrative cost; **$1.2M** is sales/rental services for housing APCHA administers but doesn't own; the bulk of the rest is facilities maintenance and sales/rental services for its five owned developments. [news — Aspen Daily News, Aug 2024, https://www.aspendailynews.com/news/apcha-has-10m-budget-for-next-year/article_bf429308-6052-11ef-bcb1-83728e7b3df9.html]
- APCHA board approved **$2.8M in 2025 requests** for the Smuggler, Truscott, and administration funds. [news — Aspen Times, https://www.aspentimes.com/news/apcha-approves-2-8-million-request-for-2025-budget-for-smuggler-truscott-administration-funds/]
- **Scale:** APCHA oversees **~3,100 deed-restricted workforce units** (largest resort workforce program in North America) and **directly manages 347 units**. [news — Aspen Daily News "Housing Matters" column, Nov 2024, https://www.aspendailynews.com/opinion/housing-matters-some-facts-about-apcha/article_d4194536-a7cb-11ef-b1dc-1f86d3c35a8d.html]
- *(derived)* Administration ≈ **$610/deed-restricted unit/yr** ($1.9M ÷ 3,100) for compliance-style oversight; total budget ≈ **$3,200/unit** across the whole portfolio — but APCHA's direct-management cost is concentrated on its 347 managed units, so its property-operations cost per managed unit is far higher (several thousand $/unit).
- **NOT FOUND:** APCHA total staff headcount (searched APCHA About page and budget press coverage twice; not published in accessible sources).

### 2(b) Whistler Housing Authority (WHA) — best per-unit benchmark found

FY2025 audited financial statements (year ended 2025-12-31) [official — https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/1211/9038/files/Whistler_Housing_Authority_2025_Financial_Statements.pdf]:

- WHA **owns 366 rental units** (unchanged from 2024) and manages 11 more for the municipality.
- FY2025 actuals: rental revenue **$7,851,056**; rental (operating) expenses **$3,476,885**; capital reserve expenses **$893,892** (budget was $1,432,140); office/admin expenses **$945,395**; repairs & maintenance within operating: **$435,991**.
- Reserve funds grew from **$1,024,241 → $1,691,746** during FY2025 (internally restricted for future capital needs of rental buildings).
- *(derived, per owned rental unit, FY2025, CAD)*: operating ≈ **$9,500/unit**; admin ≈ **$2,580/unit**; capital reserve spend ≈ **$2,440/unit** (budgeted ≈ $3,910/unit); R&M ≈ $1,190/unit. All-in expense (excl. amortization) ≈ **$14,530/unit/yr**.
- **Reserve policy (2024):** contributions tiered by building age — **25% of revenues** for older buildings, **10%** mid-age, **4%** newest. Councillors flagged ~$1M restricted reserves as thin for a 3,000-bed rental portfolio. [news — Pique Newsmagazine, 2025, https://www.piquenewsmagazine.com/local-news/whistler-housing-authority-shares-strategic-plan-and-financials-10444854]
- Context: total inventory 7,495 employee beds (3,058 rental / 4,437 ownership); 2024 surplus $11.2M, accumulated surplus $41.4M (mostly in assets/debt). [news — Pique, same]

### 2(d) Town of Telluride's own housing budget (2025 Budget Book) [official — https://telluride.gov/DocumentCenter/View/15248/2025-Town-of-Telluride-Budget-Book, adopted 2024-11-19]

- **Community Services – Housing** department: manages **185 rental units** (apartments, townhomes, tiny homes) plus the **46-bed boardinghouse**; also operates Elaine's Place childcare from 2024. 2025 approved budget **$13,756,707** (−40% vs 2024, which carried the VooDoo acquisition), including continuing Shandoka capital (Building F remodel via COPs, phase 2 concluding spring 2025).
- **By enterprise fund, 2025 approved expenditures:**
  - Shandoka Fund **$11,095,349** (80.7% — includes Building F capital; FY2023 actual was $1,732,325)
  - VooDoo Housing Fund **$1,213,938** (FY2024 was $14.4M — the acquisition year)
  - Sunnyside Fund **$820,687**
  - Virginia Placer Fund (incl. boardinghouse) **$758,244**
- *(derived)* Excluding Shandoka's capital-heavy year, steady-state operating for the smaller funds runs roughly **$700K–$1.2M per property per year**; Shandoka's pre-capital FY2023 actual of $1.73M over ~134 units ≈ **$13K/unit** all-in — in line with WHA's ~$14.5K/unit (CAD) all-in benchmark.
- **Staffing:** Shandoka/housing operations **8.48 FTE** (2024 and 2025); **Housing Development Division** (in Town Manager's office): 2 positions — Program Director and Community Housing Manager — with a division non-labor budget of **$34,500** (unchanged from 2024); development projects are separately funded from the Affordable Housing Fund, Capital Improvement Fund, and Parking Enterprise Fund.
- **Affordable Housing Fund** (1/2% sales & use tax + 2-mill property tax + 2.5% STR excise + STR regulatory fee from 2024): 2025 approved expenditures **$8,442,524**. General Fund "Housing Set Aside": **$7,097,947** budgeted 2025 (+44.9%).
- Housing enterprise funds now pay an administrative fee and insurance to the Town, raising O&M; Housing Department salaries are allocated across the four town-owned projects. Council goal: house **30%+ of Town employees** in dedicated Town units.

### Cross-cutting takeaways for the projection model

1. **WHA is the cleanest per-unit template:** ~$9.5K operating + ~$2.5K admin + $2.4–3.9K capital reserve per owned rental unit per year (CAD, FY2025), with reserve contributions tiered 4%/10%/25% of revenue by building age — apply the age-tier logic to Telluride's aging Shandoka vs. new VooDoo/Sunnyside stock.
2. **Telluride's own FY2023 Shandoka actuals (~$13K/unit all-in)** already match the mature-program benchmark; scaling to 251 units implies roughly **$3.3M/yr steady-state O&M** before major capital cycles, plus reserve funding Telluride does not yet formally tier.
3. **The WWTP is a converging cost stream:** $9.7M of 2025 wastewater capital, a $139.1M 5-yr CIP placeholder, 18% rate hikes already taken in 2025, and a new regional plant (2.67 MGD, Sewer Authority by July 2028) whose deed-restricted-housing differential rates are only aspirational MOU language so far.
