# Infrastructure Envelopes — traffic, water, wastewater (Workstream D, first pass 2026-07-31)

The carrying-capacity question (Q1) reduces to: which pipe fills first? First
answers, from the documents Morgan supplied plus the Norwood deep-dive.

## Traffic — the East End's real meter is two intersections

**Sources:** PST Engineering, Telluride Southwest Area TIS Update (Sept 4,
2025) [official]; Diamond Ridge Traffic Study, stamped FINAL (Apr 15, 2023)
[official]; Diamond Ridge TIS Exhibits N-1/N-2 [official]; Last Dollar PUD
Assn. comment letter re Society Turn PUD w/ SM Rocha review (Jan 2023)
[advocacy w/ engineer review]. Local copies: `assets/Housing Study/Traffic
studies/`.

- **In town (Sept 2025):** every studied intersection meets CDOT standards
  EXCEPT **Davis/Colorado Ave — northbound LOS F, 174-second average PM
  delay** (was 148.5s in the 2020 study; +26s in five years at ~background
  growth). Recommended fix: two-way left-turn lane or mini-roundabout
  (modeled fix restores LOS A/B). Colorado/Mahoney has real AM school-hour
  congestion the LOS tables don't capture.
- **Region (Diamond Ridge study):** Last Dollar Rd (Airport Rd)/HWY 145 Spur
  runs LOS C today and **fails on its own by 2042 at 1.5%/yr background
  growth with no new development at all** (209s delays). A 240-unit Diamond
  Ridge brings LOS E/F forward to 2027 and meets signal warrants; 780 units
  → ~9-minute waits. Diverting its traffic instead would push the **Society
  Turn roundabout to unacceptable LOS "quickly"** — the two nodes trade
  failure between them.
- **Study conventions worth reusing:** 1.5%/yr background growth; the
  Diamond Ridge engineers note Telluride runs "close-to-peak" hours all day,
  so peak-hour LOS *understates* lived congestion.
- **Implication for capacity:** the corridor's binding traffic constraint is
  not Hwy 145's pavement, it's the Society Turn RAB + Last Dollar
  intersection pair, which background growth alone degrades to failure
  inside the planning horizon. Any East End unit count must carry an
  intersection-improvement price tag (signal/RAB ≈ $2–8M class) — and the
  box canyon's single-egress evacuation question (Sanibel precedent, peer
  report) rides on the same two nodes.

## Water — Telluride is NOT the constraint; Norwood absolutely is

**Town of Telluride** (Water Efficiency Plan 2020–2027, final draft
[official]; local: `assets/Housing Study/Water/`):

- Current demand: <500 AF/yr; **2018 peak-day summer 0.90 MGD**.
- Projected demand **at full build-out of the service area: ~2.0 MGD peak
  day** (2010 Master Plan, reaffirmed 2019).
- Treatment: Mill Creek 1.0 MGD (expansion infeasible — raw supply drops to
  0.7 MGD, 0.25 MGD in runoff turbidity) + **Pandora 1.0 MGD, designed to
  double to 2.0 MGD** (needs 1-year notice to Idarado per settlement) +
  Stillwell on emergency standby. Storage rights incl. Blue Lake 920 + 413 AF.
- Plan's own conclusion: supply **adequate for current and future needs**;
  no acquisitions planned. Caveats: service area excludes most of the valley
  floor (approved development there supplies its own water), and the
  Idarado agreement freezes new upper-basin appropriations by either party.
- **Bottom line: in-town water supports roughly double today's peak use —
  water does not cap Telluride-proper housing.**

**Norwood/Wright's Mesa** (deep-dive, sourced to SGM 2020 Master Plan + Aug
2022 Supply Adequacy Memo [official]):

- Firm supply is a single contract: **300 AF/yr from Gurley Reservoir**,
  deliverable April–November only; ~198 AF already used (2022); **full
  allocation consumed ~2036 (3% growth) to ~2042 (2%)**. Pinon Park + the
  MV Employee Housing Project alone add ~124 taps (+11.6% population).
- Treatment plant (0.56 MGD) hits 92% of capacity by 2042; sole 10-inch
  transmission line is past useful life; no redundancy; 2018 drought nearly
  broke the system.
- **Bottom line: every "cheap" West End housing unit draws down a fixed
  bucket that is already two-thirds committed.** The regional-dispersal
  option (Q6) is water-limited unless the NWC firms up its decreed 5-cfs
  San Miguel River right — a major capital project.

## Wastewater — the tightest envelope (now documented)

**Source:** Telluride Regional WWTP Master Plan, final June 27, 2017
[official; local: `assets/Housing Study/Water/WWTP Master Plan Final
6-27-17.pdf`]. Serves Telluride, Mountain Village, Eider Creek, Sunset
Ridge, Aldasoro, Hillside, Lawson.

- **The binding limit is organic load (BOD5), not water volume.** Rated
  organic capacity: **3,708 lbs/day**. The plan's 2017 projected maximum
  month load: **4,380 lbs/day — already above the rating** — with the plant
  forecast to sit at 95% of permitted design capacity by 2020–2023. Load
  growth ran 12–22%/yr in 2015–16, driven by commercial growth
  (brewery/distillery waste is called out specifically) and visitor peaks.
- **Hydraulics have more room but still cross within the horizon:** rated
  ~2.1 MGD max-month; 2017 actual ~1.4–1.5; projected 2047 max-month 2.30
  MGD and peak day 3.10 MGD — over the rating before the 30-year horizon.
- **Peak population drives design:** visitors "nearly triple the
  population during peak events" — the same festival-peak physics as the
  evacuation constraint. MV's service area was already ~60% residential /
  ~90% hotel built-out in 2017.
- **Current status (verified 2026-07-31, agent pull of EPA ECHO + town
  records — see wwtp-status-and-program-cost-benchmarks.md):** permit CDPS
  CO0041840 **expired 2025-11-30 and is administratively continued**; EPA
  ECHO shows **12 of the last 13 quarters in noncompliance, 7 in Significant
  Noncompliance** (zinc, chlorine residual). The Town confirms the plant has
  hit the **80% CDPS planning trigger on BOD loading** — load-limited, as
  the 2017 plan predicted. Current flows ~0.8 MGD avg / 1.6 peak (June 2025
  MOU).
- **The expansion price discovery:** the on-site design-build expansion was
  **canceled January 2024 after 30% design priced above $133M for only
  +0.2 MGD of capacity** — and per TMV's project page, that retrofit would
  have bought capacity only **through 2047**, performed while the plant kept
  operating. The deeper problem is the site itself: **3.24 acres with only
  ~2.5 usable — "Restricted Expansion Space" is on the towns' own challenge
  list.** The 2025 town budget carries $9.7M of interim wastewater capital
  and a **$139.1M five-year CIP placeholder**. TMV also quantifies the peak
  problem: festivals push **up to 24,000 visitors** onto the system.
- **The regional fix:** a June 2025 MOU (County/MV/Telluride) targets a new
  **2.67 MGD regional plant at the Ilium/Hwy 145 Alexander parcel** ($1.1M
  land purchase, June 2024), with a new Sewer Authority required by
  **July 1, 2028**; Mountain Village carries 35% of cost/capacity. (Society
  Turn is not the active site.)
- **Bottom line: wastewater is the region's binding infrastructure
  constraint *today*, in documented regulatory noncompliance, and the exit
  costs ≥$133M — the single largest growth-driven capital item after the
  gondola.**

## Emerging capacity picture (to synthesize with Workstreams A/B)

| Envelope | Status | Binding horizon |
|---|---|---|
| Land (by-right, uninc. East End) | few hundred large-lot units; no by-right multifamily | now (structural) |
| Town of Telluride water | ~2× headroom over current peak | not binding at build-out |
| Norwood water | 300 AF, ~2/3 committed | 2036–2042 |
| Society Turn / Last Dollar intersections | LOS C→F on background growth alone | ≤2042; 2027 with one big project |
| Davis/Colorado (in-town) | LOS F now (NB movement) | now; fixable ~$1M class |
| Regional WWTP (organic/BOD5) | 80% CDPS trigger hit; permit expired/admin-continued; 7 qtrs Significant Noncompliance; fix ≥$133M (new 2.67 MGD Ilium plant, Authority due 2028-07-01) | **now** |

*Grades: traffic and Telluride water A (stamped/official studies); Norwood A
(engineer memos via deep-dive); WWTP A for the 2017 plan's numbers, C for
their applicability today pending post-upgrade verification.*
