# Fiscal & Secondary Costs of Expanding Subsidized Supply (Workstream G, first pass 2026-07-31)

**Question 4: what costs arrive WITH the units, beyond the per-unit capital
subsidy quantified in `subsidy-by-ami.md`?** Framework: five cost layers,
each sourced. Layers 1–3 are computable now; 4 awaits the APCHA/Telluride
benchmark agent; 5 is the multiplier overhead documented in the
induced-growth report.

## Layer 1 — The regional capital stack that growth draws down

Every additional household (and every additional visitor bed enabled by a
housed workforce) consumes shared systems that are at or near their ratings.
Documented replacement/expansion costs:

| System | Status | Capital figure | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regional WWTP replacement | 80% CDPS BOD trigger hit; permit admin-continued; 7 qtrs Significant Noncompliance | on-site expansion canceled Jan 2024 at **>$133M for +0.2 MGD**; new 2.67 MGD Ilium regional plant per June 2025 MOU; town CIP carries **$139.1M** 5-yr placeholder | EPA ECHO, 2025 budget, MOU [official] |
| **Gondola replacement** | built 1996; operating agreement expires **Dec 31, 2027** | **$120–150M+**, against ~$8.2M/yr of 3A revenue — a funding gap the deep dive documents; $2.46M planning budget for 2026 (SMART 50 / TOT 25 / MV+TMVOA 25) | Gondola deep dive; 3rd Supplement IGA 2026 [official] |
| Society Turn RAB / Last Dollar-145 intersection | fails on background growth ≤2042; 2027 w/ one project | signal/RAB improvements, $2–8M class [EST] | Diamond Ridge TIS [official] |
| Davis/Colorado (in-town) | LOS F now | TWLTL or mini-RAB, ~$1M class [EST] | SACP TIS Update 2025 [official] |
| Norwood second water source (San Miguel R. diversion) | required before ~2036–2042 | major capital, unpriced; plus new 0.56→? MGD treatment plant early 2030s | SGM 2020/2022 via deep dive [official] |
| Telluride water (Pandora Phase 2) | headroom exists | expansion designed-in; 1-yr Idarado notice | Water Efficiency Plan [official] |

**The point:** these are mostly *peak-sized* systems (WWTP and gondola load
scale with visitors, not just residents). New workforce housing shares these
bills and — per the induced-growth evidence — enables the visitor growth
that drives them. An honest per-unit cost should carry a pro-rata share of
this stack; a defensible convention is to attribute by population-equivalent
added (to be computed in the white paper).

## Layer 2 — Owning the portfolio: capex and balloon risk

The Town owns 200+ units (Shandoka 134, Virginia Placer 18+3, Sunnyside
~29, Boarding House 46 occupants, Voodoo 27) [official, telluride.gov/745].

- **Replacement reserves — now peer-benchmarked (grade A):** Whistler
  Housing Authority's FY2025 audited statements (366 owned rental units, the
  best mature-program comparable) run **~$9.5k operating + ~$2.6k admin +
  $2.4–3.9k capital reserve per unit per year**, with reserves tiered at
  4/10/25% of revenue by building age. Telluride's own Shandoka pre-capital
  FY2023 actual (~$13k/unit) matches the WHA all-in benchmark. Applied to
  251 units: **~$3.3M/yr steady-state O&M before reserves**, plus
  **$600k–1M/yr** of honest reserve funding. Telluride's 2025 Community
  Services–Housing budget is already **$13.76M** (Shandoka $11.1M incl.
  capital) with 8.48 housing FTE + 2 development staff [official].
- **Balloon/refinance risk already booked:** VooDoo bonds balloon **$23.05M
  Dec 2032**; Sunnyside Series 2021A/B balloon **$6.17M by 2040**
  [cost tracker, official bond docs]. These are refinancing cliffs the
  rental streams (Workstream F) cannot retire — they are deferred subsidy.
- **Vacancy carrying cost:** at the reported ~25% town-portfolio vacancy
  (Apr 2026 [news, grade C]), roughly 50 units × foregone rent ≈
  **$1.0–1.5M/yr** of budgeted revenue not arriving — a direct operating
  subsidy increase whenever pricing misses demand.

## Layer 3 — Tax-base displacement (upper bound)

Town-owned units pay no property tax. If comparable private units would
assess like the county's median attached condo ($1.35M market value → ~$4.5–
5k/yr at ~52–55 mills), 200 units ≈ **≤$1M/yr** of levy base displaced
[computed; upper bound — the honest counterfactual is murkier since the
parcels might otherwise hold fewer units or public uses; use as a bound,
not a bill].

## Layer 4 — Administration (benchmarked)

APCHA: ~$10M 2025 budget, $1.9M administration, ~3,100 units overseen (347
directly managed) [news/official mix]. WHA: ~$2.6k admin per owned unit
[official]. Telluride today: 8.48 housing FTE + 2 Housing Development staff
inside a $13.76M Community Services–Housing budget [official]. The pattern
across all three: administration scales with the portfolio and never
sunsets — roughly **$2–3k/unit/yr** at maturity.

## Layer 5 — The service-population overhead

From the induced-growth evidence [B/academic]: each housed worker household
consumes services generating roughly **15–35% additional employment
overhead** (teachers, plow drivers, clinic staff…), each fraction of which
needs housing in turn. The series converges — it is not runaway — but it
means every "100 units of need" resolves to ~115–135 units of eventual
demand at stable service levels. Budget-side, the same overhead appears as
school seats, transit service-hours, and parks/rec capacity.

## Synthesis preview

Full cost of one new 60%-AMI unit at VooDoo-class construction ≈
$1.09M capital subsidy (F) + pro-rata regional capital + $2–4k/yr reserves +
admin + service overhead — before counting the growth it accommodates. The
white paper will assemble this into a single per-unit "true cost" range and
set it against the $62–68k Vail InDEED deed-restriction alternative and
against regional dispersal (which Norwood's water clock constrains).
