It’s Story Time at Shandoka

The poles come down on the 21st. Until then, you can stand inside the proposed building and judge it for yourself.
By Morgan Smith · August 15, 2026

If you’ve driven past Shandoka this week, you’ve seen it: a thicket of lodgepoles rising out of the parking lot, strung together with orange and yellow flagging that snaps in the afternoon wind.

Those are story poles, and they may be the single most useful thing the Town has put in front of us for the Shandoka Lot redevelopment, which includes about a 900-space garage and about 50-60 housing units, among other commercial development.

What do story poles actually do?

A story pole installation is a full-scale, on-site outline of a proposed building. The vertical poles mark the corners and the height. The flagging strung between them traces the ridgelines and eaves. Stand in the lot, and you are, in effect, standing inside the building before it exists.

That matters because renderings can be deceptive, created to emphasize the positive aspects of a project. A rendering picks a flattering camera angle, a generous sky, and a vantage point no human will ever occupy, but story poles don’t. They let you answer the questions that actually determine whether a building belongs in this box canyon:

  • What happens to the view of the ski area from the surrounding buildings, the sidewalk, your kitchen window?
  • How does the roofline read against the valley walls behind it?
  • Does the mass at the street edge feel like Telluride, or like somewhere else?

You cannot get those answers from a PDF. You can get all of them in ten minutes standing in a parking lot.

The Town plans to convert the Shandoka surface lot into a mixed-use project: structured and below-grade parking to accommodate intercept, community, and visitor capacity; housing in separate buildings; a childcare facility; ground-level commercial and transit-oriented space; and reworked pedestrian, bike, and vehicle circulation. The project is in Phase 3 and is now entering formal review with HARC and Planning & Zoning.

The poles are up for a short window — roughly August 11–21. After that, the evidence comes down, and the conversation goes back to the professionally done drawings of the consultants hired by the Town to develop this project.

Design review by HARC is crucial when height, mass, scale, materials, and context are still movable and can be redesigned, but that window is closing very quickly on this project for no apparent reason.

Comments made at this stage go into the record and shape the drawings. Comments made after an approval are testimony about a decision already taken. If you have a view — enthusiastic, skeptical, or somewhere in the middle — this is the week it counts most.
