Who decides what

Seven governments share this box canyon — here's each one's lane.

Town of Telluride

Everything inside town limits: land use, housing, parks, the budget. Town Council decides; the Planning & Zoning Commission (P&Z) reviews development first; HARC guards the historic district's look.

San Miguel County

Everything outside the towns — Lawson Hill, Aldasoro, Ilium, the mesas, and the west end. The three-member Board of County Commissioners (BOCC) decides; a county Planning Commission advises.

Mountain Village

Its own town with its own council, up the hill and connected by the gondola it co-funds. Runs its own land-use process, including the Design Review Board.

Telluride School District R-1

The schools, run by an elected Board of Education. Also a major landowner in conversations about where workforce housing could go.

SMART

The San Miguel Authority for Regional Transportation — regional buses and a key player in how the free gondola gets funded.

Fire & Medical districts

The Telluride Fire Protection District (fire + EMS) and the hospital district behind the Telluride Regional Medical Center — separate elected boards, separate budgets, both public.

Plus the smaller towns — Norwood, Ridgway, Ophir, and Rico — each with its own board. Gov-Hub tracks all of them in one calendar.

Words you'll hear

The local-government decoder ring.
P&Z — Planning & Zoning Commission. Reviews development applications before they reach Town Council.
HARC — Historic & Architectural Review Commission. Signs off on how buildings in Telluride's historic district look.
BOCC — Board of County Commissioners. San Miguel County's three elected decision-makers.
PUD — Planned Unit Development. A custom zoning deal negotiated for one specific project.
LUC — Land Use Code. The rulebook for what can be built where.
Deed-restricted — housing legally reserved for local workers, priced below market.
STR — short-term rental (the Airbnb/VRBO kind). A perennial policy fight in a resort town.
Work session — a meeting for discussion only. Nothing binding gets voted on.
Agenda packet — the full stack of documents a board reads before a meeting. Public, and often the best reading in town.
IGA — intergovernmental agreement. A contract between governments — like the one that funds the gondola.
